# Regions of Western Noefra

## The Blood Fang
The Blood Fang region gets its name from the two main sub-regions: the Jungle of Fangs and the Bloodthirst Wildlands. It is the south-eastern quadrant of western Noefra, extending as far east as the eastern portion of Moon's Vengeance and as far north as the Uulan Gap and foothills of Mt. Refuge. Its western edge is the eastern side of the Three Sisters mountain range. It also includes the northern islands of the Serpent Sea and Sentinel Island, but not the more equatorial Halfway Islands.

### Jungle of Fangs
Bordered on three sides by mountains (including the volcanic Mount Refuge to the north) and the warm, shallow Serpent Sea to the south, the Jungle of Fangs is mostly jungle and steep slopes. The Jungle of Fangs is anomalously warm due to ley lines rich in elemental fire that follow the crater ring formed by the impact of the Third Moon during Moonfall.

The northern third is the most heavily forested, mostly because it has only been resettled for 80 or so years after several hundred years of laying fallow. The slopes of Mt. Refuge drain water heavily, making the very northern section swampy and boggy, but the rest is rich in rivers and lakes and is mostly unsettled. The only significant settlements here is the Autonomous Zone's capital of Sha'slar and the northern trade town of Sraasa Saat. Only the very center of the region is cleared and farmed; the surrounding jungles are largely fallow or given to steel-silk plantations. Steel-silk comes from a parasitic vine that cannot be raised in captivity and whose thorns are poisonous. When treated with (expensive and dangerous) alchemical procedures, it becomes a fiber that can be woven into cloth as light and supple as silk but as resistant to cutting as steel (hence the name). At over 50 Council Marks per yard, steel-silk is a commodity for the very wealthiest. 

The central third is split between the densely settled Tar Vol Uplands and Dolem Plain to the west of Taihu Lake (the drainage outlet for most of the northern rivers) and the lightly settled eastern jungles of the Volan and Khang Woods. Even the valleys in the western Vengeance Range are densely settled. More than half the population of the entire valley lives here, with major cities of Asai'ka (the former economic center of the entire jungle, now significantly reduced after the Red Plague), Lai'ka, Nusa, Nakhon and the independently-governed theocracy of Ar Salem Dolem, the Holy City of the Church of the Queen Ascendant. Here the ground is fertile and open. Most of the food needs of the jungle are met with grain (wheat and barley) from the plains here; more prosaic fibers like cotton and (normal) silk are produced here in quantity as well. The jungle to the west of the lake are home to spice plantations.

The southern section, around the long, deep, cool Lake Thom, is one of the areas with longest continuous settlement in western Noefra. It is much more narrow (in the northern area at least), with steeper mountains on each side. As a result, the population hugs the lake and farms rice on terraced paddies. The very southern end of the jungle opens up into dense jungle, home to most of the ophidian population.

#### Climate
The northern jungle (the Sha'slari jungle) is dense tropical rainforest. Rainfall in excess of 300 inches per year leaves the area; latent volcanic aether makes it warmer than one might expect. The end result is a pocket of dense tropical rainforest/jungle (equivalent to the Koppen/Geiger `Af` classification, similar to Earth's Amazon) trapped between mountains on three sides.

The central plains, reaching from the northern shores of Taihu Lake to the southern shore of Lake Thom and stretching almost from the bordering mountain ranges, are fertile volcanic soil. Despite being further south than the northern jungle, it is drier and (slightly) cooler, resulting in a K/G classification of Cwa. Winter temperatures in the valley rarely reach freezing, with the closest Earth analogue being northern Florida in the USA. The southern jungle, south of the end of the Wildwing Range and down to the Serpent Sea, are back to being tropical, but with a significant monsoon component, similar to northern Thailand or south-eastern China. Due to the large variations in climate, storms are violent and frequent throughout much of the year, tapering off only in the mid-to-late winter.

Both the eastern range (which bears several names, from the Vengeance Range in the north to the Queen Range near Ar Salem Dolem to the Wildwing Range in the south) and the western Outer Border Range are heavily wooded. Only the tallest peaks get any significant snow.

#### Nations
**Ar Salem Dolem.** The holy city of the Queen Ascendant, this city-state in the Vengeance Range east of Asai'ka remained independent even when Asai'ka was swallowed up by the Serpent Dominion. Consisting of the city itself, the monastery/training grounds of Tyom, the walking fortress of Ar Dolema (now semi-permanently parked about 10 miles north of the city), and the small valley to the north, its entire economy revolves around the Church. A theocracy, all of the government and social functions are provided by parts of the Priestly Bureaucracy (although many are lay members rather than ordained priests). Marginally self-sufficient due to farms in the valley, it mostly subsists on tithes paid by the faithful across the Jungle.

In recent times, there has been significant shakeups in the normally very conservative and staid bureaucracy, as a cult dedicated to the ascended former rulers of the Fanged Kingdom had infiltrated and corrupted the worship of the Queen Ascendant. This has been almost entirely rooted out by the middle of 260 AC, and the Office of Doctrine is on high alert for more evidence of corruption.

The population of ASD (who call themselves Dolemites) is about 30,000, of which 18,000 are residents of the city or Ar Dolema, which houses the Office of Doctrine. It is ruled by the three Hierarchs of the Offices of the Church: The Office of Doctrine which ensures orthodoxy and maintains historical records, while also empowering inquisitors to seek out heresy, the Office of Service, which trains and organizes the actual religious work of the priesthood, and the Office of Administration, which handles "secular" affairs, including infrastructure, defense, policing, and logistics. The Office of Finance, which has both the accountants and the tax collectors, is a major secondary force, as is the Dancers Coven, who train and organize the sacred dance which is an important part of the ceremonial life. Dancers are, often, also spies and agents in their own right, and are competent unarmed fighters.

**Serpent Dominion.** The undisputed powerhouse of the region is the Serpent Dominion. Previously the Southern District of the Stone Throne, it is one of the longest continuously-inhabited regions in Western Noefra, with Kel'al'ar (the capital) having continous habitation since the times of the Fanged Kingdom nearly 500 years ago. In the last few years, under Phraya [_aka king --ed_] Azekiel Cai'valur, it has expanded to conquer the moribund, collapsing central nation of Asai'ka. Culturally, the two were quite similar, having only diverged a few decades before with the breakup of the Stone Throne. It now occupies the entire jungle other than the free city of Tarad'am, the holy city-state of Ar Salem Dolem, and the northern Sha'slar Autonomous Zone. Probably 80% of the jungle's population falls under Phraya Cai'valur's rule.

Politically, it is a caste-based aristocracy with a strong monarch. The caste system (which separates the nobility or Isal caste from the Lai tradesmen, the unmarked common folk, and the now-eliminated untouchable Ba caste) is fading and the isal no longer have undisputed rights of high and low justice on their estates. They are still the dominant land owners and social class, although the New Money lai in and around Asai'ka City are challenging the isal for their status. Legally, only nobles of the isal (and those given life estates to act as officers and guard captains) may wear a particular shade of sapphire blue. The priestly pseudo-caste (drawn in principle from all the castes, but birth status still matters within the priesthood) have significant moral sway, especially over the common folk and the more traditional and rural isal, but lack the legal power they once had. Which loss many of them resent. The Phraya and his hand-picked courts have arrogated all jurisdiction over capital cases and allow the lai appeals-as-of-right for most lower cases. The Phraya also maintains an iron grip over the military, which has long been a separate, much-less-caste-bound, power structure.

The two major population centers are that of Kel'al'ar (urban population of 25,000, with three times that in the immediate vicinity) and Asai'ka (once much larger, now reduced to only 15,000 people but with a densely settled network of surrounding villages). Kel'al'ar is the capital, but Asai'ka remains the trade center (a role it has played since it was re-settled about 150 years ago). The outlying areas each have a local Phra (landed lord) who is sworn to the Phraya, and larger towns dot the central plains.

Religiously, the central plains are much more orthodox in their observance of the Church of the Queen Ascendant, while the south practices a simpler, less bureaucratic and more individual reformed version. Both agree on basic theology and religous precepts, but there is certainly tension (especially between the reformists and the die-hards in Ar Salem Dolem).

**Sha'slar Autonomous Zone.** The name of this nation came about during the Stone Throne era, when the fortress city of Sha'slar was the northernmost outpost of civilization and the area beyond it was under purely military jurisdiction. Occupying the northern jungle, it is still ruled by a quadrumvirate of Generals, although only two of them are really military (with the others being a noble and a rich merchant). Unlike the south, the caste system has disappeared in all but name and custom; the isal here are but land-owners (and one of the richest and most powerful is only lai) without any special rights.

There are only two settlements of any significant size: Sha'slar itself and the northern trade town of Srassa Saat. The former has a population of about 10,000 and the later only 7,000. Sha'slar is a fortress city with two rings of walls, even though the threat never materialized. Outside are warehouses and workshops where most of the raw steelsilk fiber is treated to become the finished product. Srassa Saat's economy depends on trade through the the Western Pass into Kaelthia and the Dales, as well as the (lower volume but increasingly important) trade route towards Fuar Uulan and the Uulan Gap. One major local business is the Greenscale Distillery, run by a tribe of minor dragon-kin (what many call "kobolds"). It has won wide acclaim as one of the best distilleries of hard liquor on the continent, and has been patronized by significant personages. Their top shelf whiskey is sold only in very limited quantities and commands hundreds of gold marks per 25 oz bottle.

**Tarad'am.** Legally a free, neutral city-state located at the very southern tip of the jungle, Tarad'am is a pirate town where the only rules are "don't get caught" and "don't make people care enough that they come looking for reasons to squash you." The Governor enforces these laws with an iron fist, but turns a blind eye to most things. Drugs, prostitution, stolen goods, any of this is legal. Buying and selling slaves will get you squashed, as one resident cares about it enough to enforce that rule. Two main factions are the Dimwit Syndicate, an organized crime ring that manipulates markets, fences stolen goods, contracts assassins, runs vice and gambling rings, and otherwise disturbs public order throughout the Jungle, and the Three Red Sails, a pirate crew operating on the Serpent Sea in defiance of the First Fleet. They smuggle goods, evade taxes, and run riskier cargos.

The population is highly variable, with most estimates claiming somewhere between 7 and 12 thousand people of all nationalities. In addition to the trade, many of the alchemical, drug, and dye merchants do most of their manufacturing here, especially the parts that rely on cheap labor with limited rights and no safety provisions. While slavery is illegal, many of these workers are there are on punitive indenture contracts; contracts that rarely end with them paying off the debt. The city is split between the villas of the rich on the west bank and the slums to the east (where the manufacturies are).

#### Threats and Adventures
The northern jungle is still quite unexplored in detail; many ancient (Fanged Kingdom, Old Imperial, as well as a rare Second Age Aelvar) ruins and temples hide in the hills and swamps. Monsters are not an omnipresent threat; the military keeps the settled lands fairly secure. But out in the jungle lurk all sorts of beasts, elementals, and especially fey. Plant monsters are quite common. In the northern swamps against the slopes of Mt. Refuge, the cannibal [_hey, that's just a rumor! --IB_] halfling groups strongly discourage visitors, although some have made common cause with bandit groups preying on travelers as well as running unauthorized mines (usually with slave labor taken from the local "barbarian" human tribes to the west). The western mountain folk (often called barbarians) are, if not xenophobic, at least not incredibly welcoming [_understandable, especially after Kaelthia massacred one whole tribal alliance during the brief Winter War of 202 AC for the crime of simply being in the way --IB_].

Beginning adventurers may be called on to escort caravans up the Dwarf Road, hunt for exotic plants or animals, or track beasts that threaten populations. More senior adventurers might deal with bandit coalitions or remnants of Imperial or Fanged magitech. But generally, the northern jungles are fairly safe as long as you don't go too far off the beaten track.

Most of the central plains are well-settled; monsters are rare. [_Or at least the non-humanoid variety --ed_]. But yes, as the lovely Marceline says, there are still many threats. Among these threats are corrupt nobles overstepping their bounds or dabbling in demon worship, offshoots of the Benevolent Organization, the organized crime umbrella group that once ruled Asai'ka with a silk and steel glove, as well as quests involving trying to find new and better routes through the northern Bloodthirst to Crisial (the main route there having been lost with the Red Plague). More exotic adventures might include local politics and religion; the role of religion in the area has become a more open question than it was previously. More traditional monsters lurk in the mountains, especially to the west of the lakes.

Threats in the far south mostly center around pirates at Tarad'am. Being pirates (or smugglers), fighting pirates (or smugglers), etc. More traditional threats include the semi-civilized ophidian tribes, many of whom deeply regret the loss of their snake-aspected Ascendant deities (the Coiled Ones) and harbor animosity toward the civilized folk for mistreatment over the decades.

### Sentinel Island, the Serpent Sea, and the West Moon Sea
Sentinel Island is the largest island in the western Primal Sea between Noefra and Soefra. At about 125 miles north to south and slightly more east to west, it separates the shallow Serpent Sea from the deep, wild Primal Sea. It has been inhabited on and off from the early 3rd age, but since 200 AC it has belonged to the First Fleet of the Ship Folk, who dominated its primitive human and goblin inhabitants.

The center of the island is a mountain chain, an extension of the Inner Barrier Range. Only about 2500 feet at the peaks, its convoluted valleys and ridges make it easy to hide villages and households. The remainder of the island is broad, sloping plains and light forests. The ground is fertile, making it great for intensive plantation-style agriculture.

The Serpent Sea divides the mainland (specifically the Jungle of Fangs and Dolor) from Sentinel Island. With an average depth of less than 300 feet and only two major channels, it isn't suited to large deep-water trade. The islands that dot its warm waters are small, averaging less than a mile across. Date and coconut palms and fishing are primary sources of nutrients, with rice imported from Sentinel Island rounding out the bill.

The West Moon Sea is the portion of the deep primary crater that rings Moon's Vengeance. When the Third Moon was shattered by the aelvar counterstrike, its fragments gouged out the basins of the Wast and West Moon Seas (which were at the time landlocked); when the naescent Phoenix Empire split Soefra off from Noefra, the inrushing sea filled in the gouges. Bordered on the outside by nearly-sheer mountains and on the inside by the arcane-blasted waste that is Moon's Vengeance, the Moon Seas are only sparsely traveled, especially in their northern reaches. There are no good ports on the west shore, and only the port of Gor'ka on the east. It is inhabited, however, by an isolationist group of kalasaa, ruled by an acerbic ancient bronze dragon whose use name is Icthoraz.

#### Climate
Sentinel Island is semi-tropical, but large enough that the center sees actual seasonality. Its growing season is nearly year-round, with crop rotation and careful planning resulting in 3-4 harvests per year. The Serpent Sea and borders are even warmer, basking in near-endless summer. Both experience seasonal monsoons during the late summer and early fall, coming from the south-east (following the line of the Halfway Islands). These storms so dominate the seasons that most inhabitants only really contemplate two seasons: the Dry and the Wet.

The Western Moon Sea is deep and unnaturally cold. This produces extremely violent turbulent storm fronts where the two waters meet near the north-western corner of Sentinel Island. For decades, these were thought to be impassable. Furthermore, frequent glass storms [_exactly what it sounds like, storm winds carrying shards of black arcane glass from Moon's Vengeance --ed_] sweep the eastern fringes near that Gods-blasted wasteland.

#### Nations
**First Fleet.** Formerly one of the three main subdivisions of the Ship Folk pirate nation (which controls much of the islands and sea between Noefra and Soefra), the northern First Fleet separated from the other Fleets in 255 AC. They maintain control of Sentinel Island and many of the islands in the Serpent and West Moon Sea, and ships (other than those of the Pearl fleet, which has special dispensation) seeking to trade between those islands must pay a toll to the Fleet. Ruled by the sena'ka (who maintain one of their few birthing creches on Sentinel Island), the Fleet only knows two types of people. Either you are sena'ka (by birth or honorary) and thus free, or you are a slave. But "slave" doesn't quite have the same meaning as elsewhere; slaves can be officers, they can own property and even other slaves. The only rank forbidden to a slave is captain. Elaborate chains of debt and ownership drive social relationships. 

Fleet society is rife with infighting and backstabbing, but rarely outright bad-faith betrayal (unless paid appropriately). They will generally keep their bargains, as long as it is profitable and no one else pays them more. They respect strength in many forms, including that displayed by making clever bargains. Killing a sena'ka, even in self-defense, is a heinous crime, so ransom is the order of the day. In fact, there are professional ransom go-betweens and you can purchase ransom insurance.

Sentinel Island is populated by a motley mix of mainland humans and goblins, with a few kalasaa and ika-ika thrown in in some parts. Since working the land is considered lower-class to the sea-loving sena'ka, most of the plantations here are run by nominal slaves or honorary sena'ka, who are often more brutal to their fellows than the real sena'ka are. Some of the goblins of the central mountain spine have liberated themselves, which is tolerated as long as they don't cause too many troubles for the great rice, dye, spice, and drug plantations of the lowlands.

The two main ports here are Heu Bai in the north, from which the Fleet controls the Serpent Sea, and Kong Heu on the south-west coast which acts as the primary port for ships coming from the Halfway Islands and parts south. By mainland standards, neither of these are large cities. Heu Bai is only about 5,000 people, and Kong Heu is only about 7,500.

The ruler of the First Fleet is Fleet Admiral Poer'la, a severe sena'ka woman in her late 40s. She has an uneasy alliance with Diligas Husty of Enigma; uneasy because Dil has promised to burn her fleet to the waterline if she allows raiding of the mainland or taking slaves from ships in the Serpent Sea. In return, the First Fleet operates more or less with impunity in these waters. So far she has kept this bargain and been a moderating influence, which is what led to the schism with the main Ship Folk population.

### The Bloodthirst Wildlands
The Bloodthirst is the common name for the great peninsula that separates Gap-Tooth Bay from the Jungle of Fangs. This primal, mostly untamed wilderness has resisted conquest and settlement since the end of the Second Age. Divided into three major sections: The Bloodthirst proper is the northern jungle, home to goblins, feral shifters, and therianthropes, as well as many strange beasts. The great plain of Dolor, once jungle as well but killed off when the Catalysts dropped a meteor on the ancient temple at its heart to cleanse the Twisted corruption there, is the central section, home to beast-human hybrids, insectoid monstrosities, and the great tree Cobaith, called Hope, under whose branches a zone of peace encompases many groups of people. At the far south is Cape Dread, whose rocky and reef-ridden shores are mostly inaccessible via sea. The only major habitation here is the city of Glorious Waters.

#### Nations
**Freeport.** Founded in 249 AC as a gateway to the "riches of the Bloodthirst", Freeport is located on the north-eastern shore of the Gap-Tooth Bay, west of the Three Sisters and north of the Ol-aka river mouth. Operated by the Freeport Company, it caters to adventurers seeking to make their fortune in the Bloodthirst. The Company is a shareholder-run organization chartered in Delphni. Adventurers can buy shares for a variable price (250 gp per share as of 260 AC). In return, they get reduced-price access to services in Freeport, as well as one share (currently 1/1000th) of the profit brought in by the Company. All the services in Freeport are owned by the Company (or at least pay taxes to the Company). The only other power base in Freeport are the free farmers, who are protected by the Company and provide most of the town's food, but are not shareholders themselves. They want the town to focus on proper growth and defenses, while the Company is focused on helping adventurers extract wealth (and pay it to the Company).

The current Company CEO is a human (night's child) man by the name of Alexandros. He claims to have been an adventurer before becoming injured, but records of his exploits are strangely hard to find. The current leader of the farmers is a night's child woman named Salithra, whose children and grand-children are farmers. One of her grandchildren, a girl named Iris, has run away and become an adventurer, which contributes to Salithra's grievances.

About a day's journey NE of Freeport is a great tree-spirit who goes by the name Neelix Red Oak. This ancient ihimen has taken a tribe of refugee goblins under his protection. Relatively (for an ihimen Great Spirit) friendly, his grove is a place of protection and rest. The goblins fled persecution by "an evil therianthropic empire" back in 250 AC. The threat of this supposed empire has not manifested as of this point. 

**Glorious Waters.** Once home to a nest of advanced, kraken-worshiping mkhulu who performed experiments to artificially induce worship, GW was freed by Enigma in 250 AC. Since then, it has struggled to find its path in life. The folks here (roughly 2500 of them) are a mix of formerly-enslaved sailors from the First Fleet and other ships who dared get too close, goblins and shifters from the Cape (also formerly enslaved), and a few folks that have moved there since the liberation, including some dissident kalasaa and ika ika, as well as humans and others from both Byssia and the Jungle nations. The former slaves suffered from extreme brainwashing that left them bereft of much of their knowledge of sociology and normal societal structures.

Their government is (currently) a representative democracy, with leaders chosen by sortition (random selection). They're effectively speed-running sociological development, and as such, things are a bit abnormal. Governments last until someone gets bored, annoyed, or otherwise quits. Current record is 16 months. As a result, the government is fairly ineffectual and most things are done by groups of people who see a need and act.

Notable people include Fredward, the "tame" mkhulu. He disavowed the normal brain-eating, manipulative ways (in part because he was always a bit of an outcast among his kin) and plays the role of advisor and color commentator. He's resigned to the fact that no one really listens to him, so he sits in his tower and snarks. Can be helpful if approached properly, however.

### Moon's Vengeance
This peninsula was once the most glorious, magical place on Quartus. The former home of the aelvar arcane empire now lies shattered, with the land turned to black glass. Very little life exists on the surface of the peninsula other than arcane elementals and other constructs of wild magic. But the peninsula is riddled with deep trenches, caverns, and tunnels, and life survives under the hostile surface. Life is not easy here; neither are the inhabitants.

Very little is known to the outside world about the societies and ecology here. Only two settlements have made contact with the outside world: the former pirate base Gor'ka (population about 600 souls) and a small village of mystic goroesi (the so-called dark elves) named Yondar (population under 100). What is known is that the dominant lineage here is those goroesi mentioned. Thought to have gone extinct at Moon's Fall at the end of the 2nd Age, the goroesi visually quite unique (although recognizably elven in heritage). The most distinctive features are the goroesi's skin, hair, eyes, and ears. Their skin, the source of the common name, is grey, ranging from a light ash grey to an ink-black without trace of other color. Their skin tone is cool, with more blue than red. Their eyes, like those of the gwerin, are jewel toned, but this color is uniform throughout the eyeball and their eyes lack visible pupils. Furthermore, their eyes have a faceted appearance, almost like an insect’s eyes. Sapphire blue and amethyst purple are the most common colors, but almost any pure jewel tone is found. Their hair is light and straight and has a metallic sheen. Usually metallic white or silver, but other shades are also not uncommon. Their ears, while pointed like those of the gwerin, are longer and stick out at a large angle to their angular skulls. All told, a goroesi can only pass for a member of another race while hooded and gloved with no skin showing.

They were created to serve as skilled craftsmen for the aelvar, and their primary civilization was located in a cavern below the capital city. Other groups were located under mountains to the south, surviving in Soefra to this day. At Moon's Fall, they engaged in a risky gambit, summoning and bargaining with the so-called Broken Concept of Causality to shield them by taking their cavern home out of space and time. For them, only a few generations passed while trapped in this impenetrable bubble. To the outside world, over four thousand years passed. They re-emerged around the time of the Cataclysm, and have been slowly expanding outward, fighting the unrelenting hostility of their homeland. They still worship this Concept, now become the Archon of Time; this gives some of them minor prophetic powers at the cost of much of their lifespan and their sight. These Seers are the center of their culture. While the goroesi are highly matriarchal, they do not necessarily mistreat the males. But since only women (young girls, actually) can become Seers, women have the dominant role.
